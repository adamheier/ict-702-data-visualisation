## 2. Key findings for the report

### The tension that carries the story
The DGV reports a record for 2025: 695,617 registered memberships, up 8,909 (+1.3%).
The age and regional structure shows that this growth rests on a narrow base.

### Finding 1 — Growth is demographically displaced, not broad
| Segment | 2022 | 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ages 19–40 | 103,071 | 121,811 | **+18.2%** |
| Ages 41–60 | 247,265 | 224,855 | **−9.1%** |

The 41–60 cohort — traditionally the highest-spending membership segment — lost more than
22,000 members in three years. Gains among younger adults only just offset this.

### Finding 2 — The female share is falling steadily
36.1% (2020) → 34.7% (2025), a decline of 1.41 percentage points.
In absolute terms the number of female members has stagnated at around 241,000, while male
membership grew by more than 38,000. Essentially all net growth since 2020 came from men.

### Finding 3 — An ageing membership
46.8% of all registered players are aged 60 or over. Only 6.2% are under 19.
Junior numbers in 2025 (42,798) remain below the 2016 level (43,953).

### Finding 4 — Extreme regional variation
Players per 1,000 population (2025):

| State golf association | Density | Change 2022–25 |
|---|---|---|
| Schleswig-Holstein | 18.4 | +1.2% |
| Mecklenburg-Vorpommern | 11.9 | +1.8% |
| Hamburg | 11.9 | +1.0% |
| Bayern | 10.9 | +0.5% |
| Niedersachsen/Bremen | 9.0 | +6.2% |
| Nordrhein-Westfalen | 8.0 | +1.4% |
| Hessen | 7.9 | −2.3% |
| Baden-Württemberg | 6.8 | −2.2% |
| Rheinland-Pfalz/Saarland | 5.9 | −2.4% |
| Berlin/Brandenburg | 4.5 | +1.0% |
| Sachsen/Thüringen | 2.2 | +3.7% |
| Sachsen-Anhalt | 1.0 | +1.1% |

National average: 8.3 per 1,000. Schleswig-Holstein reaches 18 times the density of Sachsen-Anhalt.
Berlin/Brandenburg is the most striking case: 6.2 million residents, but only 4.5 players per 1,000
and just 19 golf facilities.

### Finding 5 — Supply is contracting slightly
721 golf facilities in 2025, down 5 on 2024; DGV member clubs 823, down 9.
The DGV notes that some of this reflects reclassification of its own records rather than closures —
worth stating as a limitation.

### Finding 6 — Competitive position
The DGV is the eighth-largest sports federation in Germany (686,708 members, as at 1 January 2025).
Its growth of +0.7% trails football (+3.9%), gymnastics (+3.6%) and handball (+3.6%).
Golf is therefore growing more slowly than organised sport overall.

## 3. Candidate recommendations (Criterion 4)

1. **Prioritise programmes for women.** The female share has fallen for years; this is the largest
   untapped segment. Returning to 36% by 2030 would add roughly 9,000 memberships.
2. **Investigate attrition in the 41–60 cohort.** A loss of 22,000 members is the single largest
   negative item. Exit surveys and more flexible membership models are the obvious levers.
3. **Steer capacity investment by density, not absolute numbers.** Berlin/Brandenburg and
   Sachsen/Thüringen combine large populations with low penetration.
4. **Stabilise the junior pipeline.** Under-19 membership sits below its 2016 level; without
   intervention the ageing profile will worsen.
5. **Study what Niedersachsen/Bremen is doing.** At +6.2% it is by far the strongest regional performer.

## 4. Data quality notes

- Every table was reconciled against the published row and column totals. Age band by gender sums
  exactly to the published annual total for each year, and the 13 state rows sum to 695,617 (2025)
  and 682,942 (2022).
- **VcG** (Vereinigung clubfreier Golfspieler, 32,698 members) is a nationwide membership category,
  not a region. Exclude it from regional comparisons and density metrics.
- The "60 and over" table (325,743) overlaps with the "61 and over" band (306,153). Do not add them.
- Destatis population figures are based on the 2022 Census from 2024 onward and are only partly
  comparable with earlier years.
- "Registered membership" is not the same as "person who plays golf": multiple memberships and casual
  players without club affiliation are not captured. State this as a limitation.
